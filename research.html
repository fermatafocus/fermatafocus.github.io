Our Research


Not all music supports focus.

We tested how different types of music impact reading comprehension.

Our Study Design

Participants completed short reading comprehension tasks under four music conditions. We controlled for genre/tempo and used passages with similar length and difficulty.

Our results

Comprehension by condition. Instrumental and no-music conditions produced the highest reading comprehension, while English lyrics produced the lowest.

Self-reported experience. Focus and concentration drop as lyrics become more cognitively demanding, while distraction and effort rise.

How measures relate. Higher distraction and cognitive effort are associated with lower focus/concentration and lower comprehension.

Completion time. English lyrics increased time-on-task the most compared to the other conditions.

Takeaway

Comprehension declined most when participants listened to lyrics they understood, suggesting semantic (language) processing competes with reading.

Why Lyrics Interfere

Reading relies on language processing. Lyrics also require language processing. When both happen at the same time, cognitive load increases and comprehension can drop— especially when the lyrics are semantically understood.

What This Means for Students

  • choose instrumental or silence for reading-heavy tasks
  • avoid native-language lyrics when comprehension matters
  • foreign lyrics may be less disruptive than understood lyrics
  • match your music to your task type
